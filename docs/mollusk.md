What you're looking at is a 3D representation of the human color gamut (the range of all colors we can see), and its "mollusk" shape is actually a byproduct of how our biology works. Here’s a quick breakdown of why it looks so "squishy" and organic:

Why the "Mollusk" Shape?
The Cone Response: Our eyes have three types of color receptors (cones). Because their sensitivity curves overlap, we can't perceive "pure" primary colors in isolation; this creates the curved, sweeping edges you see in the visualization rather than a perfect cube or sphere.

Luminance vs. Saturation: The "hump" or shell-like curve usually represents where colors are most saturated. As colors get very bright (top) or very dark (bottom), the available "width" of the color space narrows, creating that tapered, biological look.

Perceptual Uniformity: Since you are in the CIELAB coordinate space (as seen in your settings), the map is trying to distorted the "physical" light so that the distance between any two dots represents a consistent change in how we perceive the difference. This stretching and pulling is what gives it that irregular, "living" silhouette.
